ABOUT MENGO SENIOR SCHOOL (MSS)
================================================================================
Mengo Senior School, founded in 1895, is Uganda's oldest secondary school, 
guided by the core values of Fearing God, respecting persons and property, 
and integrity, with the mission to provide quality, holistic education that 
prepares students for service in church, state, and society.

CORE VALUES
================================================================================
1. Fearing God – Inspiring students to live spiritually disciplined and 
   morally upright lives

2. Respect for Persons and Property – Encouraging students to value themselves, 
   others, and communal assets

3. Integrity – Promoting honesty, accountability, and ethical conduct in all actions

MISSION AND AIM
================================================================================
The mission of Mengo Senior School is to provide quality education through 
practical skills, teamwork, self-reliance, and nurturing God-fearing individuals.

This mission ensures a balance between academic excellence and moral development. 
The overarching aim of the school is to produce competent human resources 
capable of serving the Church, state, and global community.

The school targets holistic development by integrating academic subjects, 
vocational training, technical education, and extracurricular activities to 
prepare students for real-world challenges.

BRIEF HISTORY
================================================================================

Foundation (1895):
The school was established by the Church Missionary Society (CMS) as an 
elementary mixed school called Kayanja. Miss Chadwick, a missionary, began by 
teaching reading, writing, and prayers to local youth, mainly children of 
missionaries' houseboys.

Early Development:
As enrollment grew, students were housed temporarily on Namirembe Hill, 
graciously provided by Sir Apollo Kaggwa, the Katikkiro of Buganda. The 
potential of formal education was recognized as a way to prepare youth for 
both secular and religious leadership.

Establishment of Mengo High School (1904-1905):
Formal structures comprising classrooms and dormitories were built. The school 
was officially named Mengo High School to serve as a boarding institution for 
sons of chiefs and future leaders.

Expansion and Modernization:
Due to increasing demand, the school expanded through the early 20th century, 
separating junior and senior sections and evolving into a comprehensive mixed 
day school offering general academics, technical, vocational, and business 
subjects.
